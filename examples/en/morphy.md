# The Opera Game — Paul Morphy (Paris, 1858)

A masterclass in development, piece activity, and a checkmate delivered against
two opponents at the same time. Morphy, playing White against Duke Karl of
Brunswick and Count Isouard at the Paris opera house, never moves a piece twice
before the 10th move — and finishes with a queen sacrifice and a rook mate on the
back rank.

## Why it is a lesson

- Every White piece is developed and active before the attack starts.
- Black wastes time: the queen shuffles, the bishop is traded early, the king never castles.
- The final combination — 16. Qb8+! Nxb8 17. Rd8# — sacrifices the queen so the rook delivers checkmate.
